\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Fiber-product identification}

\begin{verbatim}
artifacts/json/native_g60_full_group_fiber_product_043.json
\end{verbatim}

This receipt compares the complete native group with the three
nontrivial quotient characters
\[
D_8\longrightarrow C_2.
\]

The character with cyclic kernel $C_4$ fails both the full element-order
profile and the index-two subgroup census.

The two characters with Klein four kernel pass. They are exchanged by
an automorphism of $D_8$ and define the same abstract fiber-product
class.

The resulting classification is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G60)
\cong
S_5\times_{C_2}D_8,
\]
where the sign character of $S_5$ is matched with a quotient character
of $D_8$ having kernel $V_4$.

\subsection{Claim boundaries}

The receipts establish a theorem about one exact finite graph and its
automorphism group.

They establish:

\begin{itemize}
\item the exact graph automorphism count;
\item the invariant quotient-fiber system;
\item the internal group invariants;
\item the abstract fiber-product type;
\item an explicit group isomorphism.
\end{itemize}

They do not establish:

\begin{itemize}
\item a physical interpretation of the graph;
\item uniqueness among all sixty-vertex quartic graphs from numerical
invariants alone;
\item a geometric interpretation of every $D_8$ coordinate;
\item any claim about forces, spacetime, quantum mechanics, or
cosmology.
\end{itemize}

Those questions lie outside the classification proved in this paper.
