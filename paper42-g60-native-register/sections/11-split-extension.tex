\section{The Split Extension}

The canonical five-point register gives an exact sequence
\[
1
\longrightarrow
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\overset{\rho}{\longrightarrow}
S_5
\longrightarrow
1.
\]
This section determines the extension type. We prove that it splits,
classify the recovered complements, and identify the conjugation action
of \(S_5\) on the native Klein group.

\subsection{Lifts of the five-point action}

Every element of the quotient group \(S_5\) has exactly four lifts to
the full automorphism group.

Indeed,
\[
\ker\rho=V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\]
has order four, so every fiber of \(\rho\) is a coset of the native
Klein group.

The complete lift profile is therefore
\[
4:120:
\]
each of the \(120\) permutations of the five matching blocks has four
native realizations.

\begin{proposition}
For every
\[
\sigma\in S_5,
\]
the fiber
\[
\rho^{-1}(\sigma)
\]
contains exactly four automorphisms of \(G_{60}\).
\end{proposition}

A splitting requires one coherent selection of a lift for every
\(\sigma\), closed under multiplication.

\subsection{Generator-lift search}

Choose the standard generators
\[
t=(0\,1)
\]
and
\[
r=(0\,1\,2\,3\,4)
\]
of \(S_5\).

There are four native lifts of \(t\) and four native lifts of \(r\).
Thus there are
\[
4\cdot4=16
\]
candidate lift pairs.

The orders of the transposition lifts are
\[
4,2,2,4,
\]
while the orders of the five-cycle lifts are
\[
5,10,10,10.
\]

For each of the sixteen pairs, we generate the subgroup of
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})\) determined by the two selected lifts.

The generated subgroup orders have profile
\[
120:2,
\qquad
240:6,
\qquad
480:8.
\]

Exactly two lift pairs generate subgroups of order \(120\).

\begin{proposition}
Among the sixteen lift pairs of the selected transposition and
five-cycle generators, exactly two generate subgroups of order
\(120\) that map surjectively onto \(S_5\).
\end{proposition}

The other lift choices retain nontrivial portions of the native kernel
and generate larger subgroups.

\subsection{Existence of a complement}

Let \(H\) be either of the two generated subgroups of order \(120\).

Direct comparison gives
\[
H\cap V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}=\{1\}.
\]

Because
\[
|\rho(H)|=120,
\]
the restriction
\[
\rho|_H:H\longrightarrow S_5
\]
is an isomorphism.

Furthermore,
\[
|H|\cdot|V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}|
=
120\cdot4
=
480
=
|\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})|.
\]

Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
=
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}H.
\]

\begin{theorem}
The exact sequence
\[
1
\longrightarrow
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\longrightarrow
S_5
\longrightarrow
1
\]
splits.
\end{theorem}

Equivalently, the full automorphism group contains a subgroup
\[
H\cong S_5
\]
that intersects the native Klein kernel trivially and projects
isomorphically onto the canonical five-point action.

\subsection{The two complements}

The search finds two distinct complements:
\[
H_0\cong S_5,
\qquad
H_1\cong S_5.
\]

They form one conjugacy orbit of size two under the full automorphism
group.

For each complement,
\[
|N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})}(H_i)|=240.
\]

Hence the number of conjugates is
\[
\frac{480}{240}=2.
\]

\begin{theorem}
The native extension contains exactly two complements in the
conjugacy class recovered by the lift search. They are exchanged by
the full automorphism group.
\end{theorem}

The visible \(S_5\)-action therefore has two equivalent native
sections.

The audit proves the two-element conjugacy orbit generated from the
recovered complements. The stronger statement that no other
nonconjugate complement class exists is not required for the splitting
theorem and is not promoted here without a complete subgroup census.

\subsection{Conjugation on the native Klein group}

Because the native Klein group is normal, every complement acts on it
by conjugation.

The full automorphism census gives exactly two conjugation signatures.

For \(240\) automorphisms,
\[
a\mapsto a,
\qquad
b\mapsto b,
\qquad
ab\mapsto ab.
\]

For the other \(240\),
\[
a\mapsto a,
\qquad
b\mapsto ab,
\qquad
ab\mapsto b.
\]

The distinction agrees exactly with the parity of the induced
five-point permutation.

If
\[
\rho(g)\in A_5,
\]
then
\[
gag^{-1}=a,
\qquad
gbg^{-1}=b,
\qquad
g(ab)g^{-1}=ab.
\]

If
\[
\rho(g)\notin A_5,
\]
then
\[
gag^{-1}=a,
\qquad
gbg^{-1}=ab,
\qquad
g(ab)g^{-1}=b.
\]

No failures occur in the complete \(480\)-element test.

\begin{theorem}[Sign action]
The conjugation action of the canonical quotient \(S_5\) on the native
Klein group factors through the sign homomorphism
\[
\operatorname{sgn}:S_5\longrightarrow C_2.
\]
Every element fixes \(a\). Even permutations fix \(b\) and \(ab\)
separately, while odd permutations exchange them.
\end{theorem}

Thus the action homomorphism
\[
S_5
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Aut}(V_4)
\cong S_3
\]
has image of order two.

\subsection{Semidirect-product description}

The splitting and sign action give the internal group description
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\cong
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}\rtimes_{\operatorname{sgn}}S_5.
\]

Here the notation means that \(S_5\) acts on
\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}=\{1,a,b,ab\}
\]
through the quotient
\[
S_5/A_5\cong C_2,
\]
whose nontrivial element fixes \(a\) and exchanges \(b\) with \(ab\).

\begin{theorem}
The full native automorphism group is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\cong
V_4\rtimes_{\operatorname{sgn}}S_5.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

This product is not direct. The odd half of \(S_5\) acts nontrivially
on the native Klein kernel.

The element \(a\) remains central, while \(b\) and \(ab\) do not.

\subsection{Centralizer of the native Klein group}

The kernel of the conjugation action consists of all automorphisms
whose five-point permutation is even.

It has order
\[
240.
\]

Under the semidirect-product model, it is
\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}\times A_5,
\]
because \(A_5\) acts trivially on the native Klein group.

\begin{corollary}
The centralizer of the native Klein group is
\[
C_{\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})}(V_4^{\mathrm{nat}})
\cong
V_4\times A_5
\]
and has order \(240\).
\end{corollary}

This recovers the index-two centralizer found directly from the native
conjugation census.

\subsection{Equivalence with the fiber-product model}

The earlier automorphism paper proves
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\cong
S_5\times_{C_2}D_8,
\]
where
\[
\chi_{V_4}:D_8\longrightarrow C_2
\]
has Klein-four kernel.

The present semidirect-product model is equivalent to that
fiber-product description.

To see the relation, let
\[
V_4=\ker\chi_{V_4}\leq D_8.
\]
Choose an element
\[
r\in D_8
\]
with
\[
\chi_{V_4}(r)=1.
\]

Conjugation by \(r\) fixes one nontrivial element of \(V_4\) and
exchanges the other two. This is the same order-two action used in
\[
V_4\rtimes_{\operatorname{sgn}}S_5.
\]

The fiber-product condition
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\chi_{V_4}(d)
\]
ensures that the nontrivial action on \(V_4\) occurs exactly when
\(\sigma\) is odd.

\begin{proposition}
There is an abstract isomorphism
\[
S_5\times_{C_2}D_8
\cong
V_4\rtimes_{\operatorname{sgn}}S_5.
\]
\end{proposition}

The two descriptions emphasize different structures.

The fiber-product model emphasizes compatible quotient characters:
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\chi_{V_4}(d).
\]

The semidirect-product model emphasizes the native kernel and its
visible action:
\[
a\mapsto a,
\qquad
b\leftrightarrow ab
\]
under odd permutations.

\subsection{What the splitting earns}

The splitting converts the quotient \(S_5\) from an abstract factor
group into an actual subgroup acting on the native graph.

It proves that every automorphism may be expressed as
\[
hg,
\qquad
h\in V_4^{\mathrm{nat}},
\quad
g\in H\cong S_5.
\]

The expression is unique after one complement \(H\) is selected,
because
\[
H\cap V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}=\{1\}.
\]

The visible and hidden coordinates are therefore separable as a set,
but their multiplication remains coupled by parity.

This is the exact algebraic realization of the native register:

\[
\boxed{
\text{hidden }V_4
\quad\rtimes_{\operatorname{sgn}}\quad
\text{visible }S_5.
}
\]

\subsection{Boundary}

The sign action is a theorem about finite graph automorphisms and
permutation parity.

It is not identified here with a physical parity operation, charge,
spin, time reversal, or any other physical observable.

The established statement is:
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\rho(g))
\]
determines whether the native automorphism fixes or exchanges the
\(b\)- and \(ab\)-directions.
