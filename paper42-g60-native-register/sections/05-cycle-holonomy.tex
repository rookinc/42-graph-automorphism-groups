\section{Cycle Holonomy}

The voltage labels on individual quotient edges depend on the chosen
chamber representatives. Their products around closed quotient cycles
do not. This section identifies the resulting gauge-invariant return
character and determines its first nontrivial occurrence.

\subsection{Holonomy of a quotient cycle}

Let
\[
Q=G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P),
\]
and let
\[
C=(v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_{\ell-1},v_0)
\]
be an oriented cycle in \(Q\).

For a chosen voltage assignment
\[
\omega:E(Q)\longrightarrow V_4,
\]
define the holonomy of \(C\) by
\[
\operatorname{hol}(C)
=
\omega_{v_0v_1}
\omega_{v_1v_2}
\cdots
\omega_{v_{\ell-1}v_0}.
\]

Because \(V_4\) is abelian and every element is its own inverse,
reversing the orientation does not change the holonomy.

Under a gauge change
\[
\omega_{ij}'=h_i\omega_{ij}h_j,
\]
the factors \(h_i\) cancel around the closed cycle. Hence
\[
\operatorname{hol}'(C)
=
\operatorname{hol}(C).
\]

\begin{proposition}
Cycle holonomy is independent of the selected chamber
representatives. It is an intrinsic \(V_4\)-valued invariant of a
closed quotient cycle.
\end{proposition}

Holonomy records the hidden state accumulated after one visible return
to the starting quotient chamber.

\subsection{Lift behavior}

Choose a native vertex \(x\) above \(v_0\). Lifting one traversal of
\(C\) beginning at \(x\) ends at
\[
\operatorname{hol}(C)\,x.
\]

If
\[
\operatorname{hol}(C)=1,
\]
one quotient traversal closes in the native graph.

If
\[
\operatorname{hol}(C)\neq1,
\]
one traversal returns to the same quotient chamber but to a different
native state. Since every nonidentity element of \(V_4\) has order
two, a second traversal closes.

Thus the visible and native return lengths are
\[
\ell
\quad\text{and}\quad
2\ell,
\]
respectively, for nontrivial holonomy.

\begin{proposition}
A quotient cycle of trivial holonomy lifts to cycles of the same
length. A quotient cycle of nontrivial holonomy lifts to cycles of
twice its quotient length.
\end{proposition}

We call the second case \emph{registered closure}: the quotient
position closes after one traversal, while the native state closes
only after two.

\subsection{Bounded quotient-cycle census}

Simple quotient cycles were enumerated through length eight. The audit
contains
\[
557
\]
cycles in total, with holonomy profile
\[
1:25,
\qquad
a:220,
\qquad
b:156,
\qquad
ab:156.
\]

The profile by length is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
\text{length} & 1 & a & b & ab\\
\hline
3 & 10 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
5 & 0 & 0 & 6 & 6\\
6 & 0 & 10 & 30 & 30\\
7 & 0 & 60 & 60 & 60\\
8 & 15 & 150 & 60 & 60
\end{array}
\]

The ten quotient triangles all have trivial holonomy. Therefore the
shortest nontrivial return character occurs at length five.

\begin{theorem}
The shortest simple quotient cycles with nontrivial native holonomy
are pentagons.
\end{theorem}

The bounded census is sufficient for this minimality statement because
all shorter simple cycles in the quartic quotient are included.

\subsection{Pentagon holonomy}

The quotient contains exactly twelve pentagons in the audited
coordinate system. Their holonomy profile is
\[
b:6,
\qquad
ab:6.
\]

No quotient pentagon has holonomy \(1\) or \(a\).

\begin{theorem}
The twelve quotient pentagons divide into two equal holonomy families:
six \(b\)-pentagons and six \(ab\)-pentagons.
\end{theorem}

This division reproduces the native character grammar
\[
a\mid\{b,ab\}.
\]
The fixed element \(a\) does not occur as pentagon holonomy, while the
exchanged pair \(b,ab\) occurs with equal multiplicity.

\subsection{Lifted pentagons}

Each nontrivial quotient pentagon lifts to exactly two disjoint native
ten-cycles.

Indeed, a pentagon contains five quotient chambers, each with four
native vertices. The full preimage therefore contains twenty vertices.
The two lifted ten-cycles are disjoint and together cover all twenty
vertices above the pentagon.

\begin{theorem}
Every quotient pentagon lifts to two disjoint native ten-cycles.
Together the two cycles cover exactly the twenty native vertices above
the five quotient chambers.
\end{theorem}

For each lifted cycle,
\[
5
\]
steps complete the visible quotient circuit, while
\[
10
\]
steps complete the native return.

This gives the exact relation
\[
\boxed{\text{visible closure }5
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{registered closure }10.}
\]

\subsection{Exchange symmetry}

The full automorphism group contains elements exchanging
\[
b\longleftrightarrow ab.
\]
These automorphisms exchange the two pentagon-holonomy families.

Consequently, the equality
\[
6_b=6_{ab}
\]
is symmetry-protected. Neither family is intrinsically preferred by
the unmarked graph.

\begin{proposition}
The \(b\)- and \(ab\)-pentagon families form one exchanged pair under
the full automorphism group.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Interpretive boundary}

Holonomy is a finite covering invariant. It records the native
group element accumulated around a closed quotient path.

The following statements are established:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item local voltage labels are gauge dependent;
    \item closed-cycle holonomy is gauge invariant;
    \item the shortest nontrivial holonomy occurs on quotient
    pentagons;
    \item those pentagons have holonomy \(b\) or \(ab\);
    \item each lifts to two native ten-cycles.
\end{enumerate}

No physical transport, energy, time, or force interpretation is
required by these results. The word ``return'' refers only to closure
of a finite graph path in quotient and native coordinates.
