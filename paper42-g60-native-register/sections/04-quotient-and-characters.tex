\section{The Quotient and Its Character Operators}

The free native Klein action produces a fifteen-vertex quotient and a
four-sector character decomposition of the native state space. This
section constructs both objects and separates gauge-dependent voltage
coordinates from gauge-invariant spectral data.

\subsection{The quotient \(G_{60}/V_4\)}

Let
\[
\pi:V(G_{60})\longrightarrow V(G_{60})/V_4
\]
be the orbit projection for the free native Klein action.

Since every orbit has size four, the quotient has fifteen vertices.
For quotient chambers \(C_i\) and \(C_j\), adjacency is inherited from
the native graph whenever a native edge joins a vertex of \(C_i\) to a
vertex of \(C_j\).

The exact quotient construction gives a simple quartic graph on
fifteen vertices and thirty edges.

\begin{proposition}
The orbit quotient
\[
G_{60}/V_4
\]
is a connected quartic graph with
\[
|V(G_{60}/V_4)|=15,
\qquad
|E(G_{60}/V_4)|=30.
\]
\end{proposition}

The quotient is not obtained by collapsing arbitrary four-sets. It is
the orbit graph of a normal subgroup of the full automorphism group.
Accordingly, the full automorphism group acts on the fifteen quotient
chambers.

\subsection{Identification with \(L(P)\)}

The quotient graph is identified with the line graph of the Petersen
graph:
\[
G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P).
\]

Under this identification, the fifteen quotient chambers correspond
to the fifteen Petersen edges. Two chambers are adjacent precisely
when the corresponding Petersen edges share an endpoint.

\begin{theorem}
The native Klein quotient satisfies
\[
G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P).
\]
\end{theorem}

The quotient therefore inherits the standard parameters
\[
|V(L(P))|=15,
\qquad
|E(L(P))|=30,
\qquad
\deg(v)=4.
\]

This quotient is distinct from the thirty-vertex quotient
\[
G_{60}/\langle a\rangle=G_{30}
\]
used in the earlier automorphism paper. The two quotients belong to
different levels of the native subgroup tower:
\[
G_{60}
\longrightarrow
G_{30}
\longrightarrow
G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P).
\]

\subsection{Voltage coordinates}

Choose one representative
\[
s_i\in C_i
\]
from each of the fifteen native chambers.

For every quotient edge \(ij\), there is a unique element
\[
\omega_{ij}\in V_4
\]
such that the native edge above \(ij\) joins
\[
s_i
\quad\text{to}\quad
\omega_{ij}s_j.
\]

The assignment
\[
\omega:E(L(P))\longrightarrow V_4
\]
is a \(V_4\)-valued voltage description of the native graph.

With one deterministic representative choice, the sixty directed
quotient incidences have raw voltage profile
\[
1:12,
\qquad
a:8,
\qquad
b:32,
\qquad
ab:8.
\]

These counts describe one chosen local frame. They are not invariants
of the unmarked graph.

\subsection{Gauge switching}

A different representative choice has the form
\[
s_i'=h_i s_i,
\qquad
h_i\in V_4.
\]

The voltage on a quotient edge then changes by
\[
\omega_{ij}'
=
h_i\,\omega_{ij}\,h_j,
\]
where inversion is unnecessary because every element of \(V_4\) has
order at most two.

The audit tests sixty-four independent local representative changes.
All produce valid voltage descriptions of the same native graph.
Across these controls, fifty-five distinct raw voltage-count profiles
occur.

\begin{proposition}
Raw \(V_4\)-voltage labels depend on the selected chamber
representatives. Their edgewise values and global count profiles are
not invariants of \(G_{60}\).
\end{proposition}

This prevents the chosen labels \(1,a,b,ab\) on individual quotient
edges from being interpreted as intrinsic local observables.

\subsection{The four characters}

The real character group of \(V_4\) also has four elements:
\[
\widehat{V_4}
=
\{\chi_1,\chi_a,\chi_b,\chi_{ab}\}.
\]

We use the convention that each nontrivial character has kernel
generated by its subscript:
\[
\ker\chi_a=\langle a\rangle,
\qquad
\ker\chi_b=\langle b\rangle,
\qquad
\ker\chi_{ab}=\langle ab\rangle.
\]

For each character \(\chi\), define a signed quotient adjacency
operator
\[
A_\chi
\]
by
\[
(A_\chi)_{ij}
=
\begin{cases}
\chi(\omega_{ij}), & ij\in E(L(P)),\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The trivial-character operator is the ordinary quotient adjacency
matrix:
\[
A_{\chi_1}=A_{L(P)}.
\]

The corresponding character Laplacians are
\[
L_\chi=4I-A_\chi.
\]

\subsection{Character decomposition of the native state space}

Let
\[
\mathcal H=\mathbb R^{V(G_{60})}.
\]

Because \(V_4\) is abelian, the native permutation representation
decomposes into character subspaces:
\[
\mathcal H
=
\mathcal H_{\chi_1}
\oplus
\mathcal H_{\chi_a}
\oplus
\mathcal H_{\chi_b}
\oplus
\mathcal H_{\chi_{ab}}.
\]

The free action has fifteen regular \(V_4\)-orbits. Each character
therefore occurs once per chamber, giving
\[
\dim\mathcal H_\chi=15
\]
for every \(\chi\).

\begin{theorem}
The sixty-dimensional native state space decomposes orthogonally as
\[
\mathbb R^{60}
=
\mathcal H_{\chi_1}
\oplus
\mathcal H_{\chi_a}
\oplus
\mathcal H_{\chi_b}
\oplus
\mathcal H_{\chi_{ab}},
\]
with four sectors of dimension fifteen.
\end{theorem}

Under a representative choice, the restriction of the native
adjacency operator to \(\mathcal H_\chi\) is represented by
\(A_\chi\). Thus the four fifteen-dimensional operators together
recover the full native adjacency spectrum with multiplicity.

\subsection{Gauge covariance of the character operators}

Under the representative change
\[
s_i'=h_i s_i,
\]
define the diagonal sign matrix
\[
D_\chi
=
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(
\chi(h_0),\ldots,\chi(h_{14})
\bigr).
\]

The switched character operator satisfies
\[
A_\chi'
=
D_\chi A_\chi D_\chi.
\]

Similarly,
\[
L_\chi'
=
D_\chi L_\chi D_\chi.
\]

\begin{theorem}
Every local representative change acts on each character operator by
diagonal sign conjugation. Consequently, the spectrum of \(A_\chi\)
and the spectrum of \(L_\chi\) are gauge invariant.
\end{theorem}

This is the proper invariant replacement for raw voltage counts.

The local labels move with the frame. The character spectra do not.

\subsection{Character spectra}

The exact Laplacian spectra are as follows.

For the trivial sector,
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_1})
=
\{0^1,2^5,5^4,6^5\}.
\]

For the distinguished \(a\)-sector,
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_a})
=
\{1^4,4^5,6^6\}.
\]

For the exchanged sectors,
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_b})
=
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_{ab}})
\]
with
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_b})
=
\left\{
\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\right)^3,
3^4,
\left(\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}\right)^3,
6^5
\right\}.
\]

The two irrational values are
\[
\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]

These values arise as exact eigenvalues of the finite character
Laplacians. No physical interpretation is assigned to them here.

\subsection{The \(a\mid\{b,ab\}\) spectral split}

The matrices
\[
A_{\chi_b}
\quad\text{and}\quad
A_{\chi_{ab}}
\]
are not equal in the selected gauge. Nevertheless, they are
isospectral.

This equality is protected by the full automorphism group. The
conjugation action fixes \(a\) and exchanges \(b\) with \(ab\). The
same exchange carries the \(\chi_b\)-sector to the
\(\chi_{ab}\)-sector.

\begin{theorem}
The isospectrality
\[
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_b})
=
\operatorname{Spec}(L_{\chi_{ab}})
\]
is forced by native graph automorphisms. It is not an accidental
agreement of two independently constructed matrices.
\end{theorem}

The character operators therefore reproduce the intrinsic grammar
already found in the native Klein group:
\[
\chi_a
\mid
\{\chi_b,\chi_{ab}\}.
\]

\subsection{Control boundary}

Conjugated control actions retain an abstract four-sector
decomposition whenever they preserve a regular \(V_4\)-set structure.
However, the tested controls do not preserve the native edge set and
do not reproduce the exact character signatures of \(G_{60}\).

Thus two claims must remain separate:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item a regular free \(V_4\)-action generically gives four equal
    character sectors;

    \item the particular operators, spectra, holonomy, and exchange
    symmetry described here depend on the native graph.
\end{enumerate}

The first is representation-theoretic. The second is the
graph-specific theorem used in the remainder of the paper.
