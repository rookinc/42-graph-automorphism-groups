\section{The Five Petersen Matchings}

The foreign-Klein incidence graph partitions the fifteen native
chambers into five triples and completes each triple to a triangle.
This section identifies the same triples inside the carrier quotient
\[
G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P)
\]
and then interprets them through the explicit Petersen-edge coordinate
bridge.

\subsection{The five chamber triples}

The five connected components of the foreign-Klein incidence graph are
\[
T_0=\{0,5,14\},
\]
\[
T_1=\{1,6,12\},
\]
\[
T_2=\{2,9,13\},
\]
\[
T_3=\{3,7,11\},
\]
and
\[
T_4=\{4,8,10\}.
\]

Each component is complete in the foreign incidence relation:
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{for}}[T_i]\cong K_3.
\]

The five triples are pairwise disjoint and cover all fifteen chambers:
\[
\{0,\ldots,14\}
=
T_0\sqcup T_1\sqcup T_2\sqcup T_3\sqcup T_4.
\]

\subsection{Their induced structure in the carrier}

We now inspect the same five triples in the quotient carrier
\[
Q=G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P).
\]

For every \(i\), the induced carrier subgraph on \(T_i\) contains no
edges:
\[
Q[T_i]\cong\overline{K_3}.
\]

\begin{theorem}
Each foreign-Klein triangle is an independent three-set in the carrier
graph \(L(P)\).
\end{theorem}

Thus the carrier and foreign relations are complementary on each
block:
\[
Q[T_i]\cong\overline{K_3},
\qquad
\Gamma_{\mathrm{for}}[T_i]\cong K_3.
\]

Equivalently, all three internal chamber pairs are absent from carrier
adjacency and present in foreign-Klein incidence.

\subsection{The explicit Petersen coordinate bridge}

The quotient-to-Petersen coordinate bridge assigns to each chamber
index one edge of a fixed Petersen graph.

The relevant map is:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{chamber} & \text{Petersen edge}\\
\hline
0 & \{0,1\}\\
1 & \{0,4\}\\
2 & \{3,4\}\\
3 & \{2,3\}\\
4 & \{1,2\}\\
5 & \{3,8\}\\
6 & \{6,8\}\\
7 & \{6,9\}\\
8 & \{4,9\}\\
9 & \{1,6\}\\
10 & \{5,8\}\\
11 & \{0,5\}\\
12 & \{2,7\}\\
13 & \{5,7\}\\
14 & \{7,9\}
\end{array}
\]

This bridge is an editorial coordinate choice among the graph
isomorphisms between the quotient and \(L(P)\). It does not change the
abstract matching property established below.

\subsection{Three-edge matchings}

Under the bridge, the five chamber triples become:

\[
T_0
\longmapsto
M_0=
\bigl\{
\{0,1\},
\{3,8\},
\{7,9\}
\bigr\},
\]

\[
T_1
\longmapsto
M_1=
\bigl\{
\{0,4\},
\{6,8\},
\{2,7\}
\bigr\},
\]

\[
T_2
\longmapsto
M_2=
\bigl\{
\{3,4\},
\{1,6\},
\{5,7\}
\bigr\},
\]

\[
T_3
\longmapsto
M_3=
\bigl\{
\{2,3\},
\{6,9\},
\{0,5\}
\bigr\},
\]

and

\[
T_4
\longmapsto
M_4=
\bigl\{
\{1,2\},
\{4,9\},
\{5,8\}
\bigr\}.
\]

Within each \(M_i\), the three Petersen edges are pairwise
vertex-disjoint.

\begin{theorem}
Each chamber triple corresponds to a three-edge matching in the
Petersen graph.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Two vertices of \(L(P)\) are adjacent exactly when their corresponding
Petersen edges share an endpoint.

Each \(T_i\) is independent in \(L(P)\). Therefore no two Petersen
edges assigned to chambers in \(T_i\) share an endpoint. Hence the
three edges form a matching.
\end{proof}

This gives a direct explanation for carrier independence. The carrier
keeps the three chambers apart because their Petersen edges do not
meet.

\subsection{Partition of the Petersen edge set}

The five matchings contain
\[
5\cdot3=15
\]
edges in total.

All fifteen displayed Petersen edges are distinct, and the Petersen
graph itself has fifteen edges. Therefore
\[
E(P)
=
M_0\sqcup M_1\sqcup M_2\sqcup M_3\sqcup M_4.
\]

\begin{theorem}
The five chamber triples define a partition of the Petersen edge set
into five matchings of size three.
\end{theorem}

This is not a proper edge-colouring of the cubic Petersen graph. A
proper edge-colouring by five colours would impose a different
condition at vertices. The theorem here states only that the fifteen
edges are partitioned into five pairwise-disjoint three-edge
matchings.

Each matching omits four of the ten Petersen vertices. The omitted
vertex set may vary from one matching to another.

\subsection{Foreign Klein groups as internal matching pairs}

Fix one matching block
\[
M_i=\{e_0,e_1,e_2\}.
\]

Its three chambers correspond to the three Petersen edges
\[
e_0,e_1,e_2.
\]

The three foreign Klein subgroups supported on the block correspond to
the three unordered internal pairs:
\[
\{e_0,e_1\},
\qquad
\{e_0,e_2\},
\qquad
\{e_1,e_2\}.
\]

Thus each foreign Klein subgroup relates two disjoint Petersen edges
belonging to the same matching.

\begin{proposition}
The fifteen foreign Klein groups are naturally indexed by the internal
two-subsets of the five Petersen matchings:
\[
\coprod_{i=0}^4 \binom{M_i}{2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

The count is
\[
5\binom32
=
5\cdot3
=
15.
\]

This indexing concerns pairs \emph{within} a matching block. It should
not be confused with the ten unordered pairs of distinct matching
blocks:
\[
\binom52=10.
\]

\subsection{Two relations on one chamber set}

The fifteen chamber labels now support two precise graph structures.

The carrier graph is
\[
Q\cong L(P).
\]
It records endpoint incidence:
\[
e\sim_Q f
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e\cap f\neq\varnothing.
\]

The foreign graph is
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{for}}\cong5K_3.
\]
It records common membership in one recovered matching:
\[
e\sim_{\mathrm{for}}f
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
e\neq f
\text{ and }
e,f\in M_i
\text{ for some }i.
\]

Within each matching block,
\[
e\cap f=\varnothing,
\]
so carrier adjacency is absent, while foreign incidence is present.

This gives the exact statement:

\begin{quote}
The carrier separates each Petersen matching; the foreign Klein layer
completes its triangle.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Coordinate dependence and invariant content}

The displayed Petersen vertex labels
\[
0,\ldots,9
\]
depend on the selected coordinate bridge. An alternative graph
isomorphism
\[
G_{60}/V_4\longrightarrow L(P)
\]
would relabel the edges and the five matchings.

The following properties do not depend on that editorial choice:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the fifteen chambers split into five triples;
    \item every triple is independent in the quotient carrier;
    \item every triple is complete in foreign-Klein incidence;
    \item under any quotient isomorphism with \(L(P)\), every triple
    becomes a three-edge Petersen matching;
    \item the five triples partition the fifteen quotient vertices and
    hence the fifteen Petersen edges.
\end{enumerate}

The next section proves a stronger invariance statement: the full
automorphism group preserves the five-block system and acts on it as
the full symmetric group \(S_5\).
