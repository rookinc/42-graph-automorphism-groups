\section{The Native Klein Register}

The earlier automorphism classification identifies a Klein four-group
as the kernel of the projection onto the \(S_5\)-coordinate. In this
section we recover that subgroup directly as permutations of the exact
native vertex set and verify its action on \(G_{60}\).

\subsection{Recovery of the involution \(a\)}

The previously established canonical two-fold quotient
\[
G_{60}\longrightarrow G_{30}
\]
comes with a fixed-point-free deck involution \(a\). Its thirty fibers
are unordered pairs of native vertices.

The first step is to reconstruct \(a\) directly from this fiber
partition. For each fiber
\[
\{u,v\},
\]
define
\[
a(u)=v,
\qquad
a(v)=u.
\]
The resulting permutation satisfies
\[
a^2=1
\]
and has no fixed points.

The complete native edge set is preserved:
\[
\{u,v\}\in E(G_{60})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{a(u),a(v)\}\in E(G_{60}).
\]
Thus \(a\) is recovered independently as a graph automorphism from the
quotient fibers alone.

\begin{proposition}
The canonical fiber partition determines a unique fixed-point-free
involution
\[
a\in\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60}).
\]
Its orbit set has thirty blocks of size two.
\end{proposition}

The uniqueness here is relative to the displayed fiber partition. A
pair partition has exactly one block-swap permutation.

\subsection{Recovery of \(b\) and \(ab\)}

The remaining two nonidentity elements are recovered from the exact
native generator bundle used in the earlier construction. The source
bundle is hash-locked, so the recovered permutations are tied to one
fixed upstream data packet rather than inferred from group order or
cycle type.

The recovered permutations \(b\) and \(ab\) satisfy
\[
b^2=(ab)^2=1,
\]
have no fixed points, and preserve every native edge.

Permutation composition verifies
\[
ab=a\circ b=b\circ a.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\{1,a,b,ab\}
\]
is closed under multiplication.

\begin{theorem}
The four permutations
\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
=
\{1,a,b,ab\}
\]
form a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})\) isomorphic to the
Klein four-group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each nonidentity element is an involution. The permutations \(a\) and
\(b\) commute, and their product is the distinct involution \(ab\).
Hence the subgroup generated by \(a\) and \(b\) has four elements and
is isomorphic to \(C_2\times C_2\).
\end{proof}

We call this subgroup the \emph{native Klein group}. The qualifier
distinguishes it from other Klein four-subgroups that will arise later
as stabilizers of frontier cycles.

\subsection{The free action}

Every nonidentity element of \(V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}\) is fixed-point-free.
Therefore the action of \(V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}\) on the sixty native
vertices is free.

For a vertex \(v\), its orbit is
\[
[v]_{V_4}
=
\{v,a(v),b(v),ab(v)\}.
\]
Every such orbit has cardinality four. Since
\[
|V(G_{60})|=60,
\]
the orbit set has cardinality
\[
60/4=15.
\]

\begin{proposition}
The native Klein group acts freely on \(V(G_{60})\), producing exactly
fifteen disjoint four-vertex orbits.
\end{proposition}

We refer to these four-vertex orbits as the \emph{native chambers}.
Thus
\[
V(G_{60})
=
\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{14} C_i,
\qquad
|C_i|=4.
\]

This is the first structural realization of the factorization
\[
60=15\cdot4.
\]
The factor four is not an arbitrary coordinate size. It is the order
of a free normal subgroup acting on the exact graph.

\subsection{Normality in the full automorphism group}

The complete automorphism census contains \(480\) permutations. Direct
conjugation of the native Klein group by every automorphism shows that
\[
gV_4^{\mathrm{nat}}g^{-1}
=
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\]
for all
\[
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60}).
\]

\begin{theorem}
The native Klein group is normal:
\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\trianglelefteq
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60}).
\]
\end{theorem}

Normality implies that every graph automorphism permutes the fifteen
native chambers. It also makes the quotient by the full Klein group a
canonical object of the unmarked graph, rather than a quotient attached
only to one selected coordinate system.

\subsection{Conjugation on the native Klein group}

Conjugation induces a homomorphism
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Aut}(V_4^{\mathrm{nat}})
\cong S_3.
\]

The image is not the full \(S_3\). Exact enumeration gives only two
conjugation actions.

Half of the automorphisms act trivially:
\[
a\mapsto a,
\qquad
b\mapsto b,
\qquad
ab\mapsto ab.
\]

The other half fix \(a\) and exchange the remaining two involutions:
\[
a\mapsto a,
\qquad
b\mapsto ab,
\qquad
ab\mapsto b.
\]

Thus the conjugation image has order two.

\begin{theorem}
The conjugation action of
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})\) on the native Klein group has image
\(C_2\). Every automorphism fixes \(a\), and the nontrivial image
element exchanges \(b\) with \(ab\).
\end{theorem}

Equivalently, there is an exact sequence
\[
1
\longrightarrow
C_{\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})}(V_4^{\mathrm{nat}})
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})
\longrightarrow
C_2
\longrightarrow
1.
\]
The centralizer has order
\[
240.
\]

In particular,
\[
a\in Z(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})),
\]
while \(b\) and \(ab\) are not individually fixed under the full group.

\subsection{The native character grammar}

The three nontrivial elements therefore do not occupy one homogeneous
orbit under conjugation. Instead they have the intrinsic form
\[
a\mid\{b,ab\}.
\]

The element \(a\) is globally distinguished. The elements \(b\) and
\(ab\) form an exchanged pair. Any invariant of the full automorphism
group may distinguish \(a\) from the pair \(\{b,ab\}\), but it cannot
select one of \(b\) and \(ab\) without additional structure.

This conjugation grammar will reappear in three places:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the character spectra of the quotient operators;
    \item the \(b\)- and \(ab\)-holonomy families of quotient
    pentagons; and
    \item the sign action in the split extension by \(S_5\).
\end{enumerate}

The native Klein register is therefore more than a four-element deck
group. It carries one fixed direction and one exchange pair:
\[
\boxed{a\mid\{b,ab\}}.
\]
