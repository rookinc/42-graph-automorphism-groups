\section{Foreign Klein Stabilizers}

The \(120\) nonregistered cycles in the third-moment frontier form one
automorphism orbit. Their stabilizers are Klein four-groups, but they
are not the native Klein group. This section classifies those
stabilizers and determines their incidence geometry on the fifteen
native chambers.

\subsection{The frontier orbit}

Let \(\mathcal F\) denote the set of \(120\) nonregistered ten-cycles
sharing the registered adjacency moments through order three.

The full automorphism group acts transitively on \(\mathcal F\).

\begin{theorem}
The \(120\) frontier cycles form one
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})\)-orbit.
\end{theorem}

By orbit--stabilizer, every frontier cycle \(C\in\mathcal F\) has
setwise stabilizer of order
\[
|\operatorname{Stab}(C)|
=
\frac{480}{120}
=
4.
\]

\subsection{The stabilizer of one frontier cycle}

For every \(C\in\mathcal F\), the stabilizer contains the identity and
three involutions. Its element-order profile is
\[
1^1\,2^3.
\]

\begin{theorem}
Every frontier-cycle stabilizer is isomorphic to the Klein four-group:
\[
\operatorname{Stab}(C)\cong V_4.
\]
\end{theorem}

The induced action on the ten cycle vertices is faithful. For a
representative frontier cycle, the restriction profile consists of:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the identity, fixing all ten cycle vertices;
    \item two fixed-point-free involutions on the cycle;
    \item one involution fixing exactly two cycle vertices.
\end{enumerate}

This local action differs sharply from the dihedral stabilizer
\[
D_{10}
\]
of a registered cycle.

\subsection{Foreign versus native Klein groups}

Write
\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
=
\{1,a,b,ab\}
\]
for the native free Klein group, and write
\[
V_4(C)=\operatorname{Stab}(C)
\]
for the stabilizer of a frontier cycle.

Direct comparison gives
\[
V_4(C)\cap V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
=
\{1\}
\]
for every \(C\in\mathcal F\).

Moreover, no \(V_4(C)\) is conjugate to the native Klein group inside
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})\).

\begin{theorem}
Every frontier stabilizer is a foreign Klein group:
\[
V_4(C)\cap V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}=\{1\},
\]
and
\[
V_4(C)
\]
is not conjugate to
\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\]
in the full automorphism group.
\end{theorem}

The two kinds of Klein subgroup therefore have different structural
roles:

\[
V_4^{\mathrm{nat}}
\quad\text{organizes the global chamber quotient,}
\]
while
\[
V_4(C)
\quad\text{stabilizes one relay-bearing frontier cycle.}
\]

\subsection{Distinct subgroup census}

Although there are \(120\) frontier cycles, their stabilizers are not
all distinct.

The complete stabilizer census contains exactly
\[
15
\]
distinct foreign Klein groups.

Each foreign subgroup stabilizes exactly
\[
8
\]
frontier cycles. Hence
\[
120=15\cdot8.
\]

\begin{theorem}
The frontier stabilizers consist of fifteen distinct foreign Klein
four-groups, each stabilizing exactly eight frontier cycles.
\end{theorem}

The fifteen foreign groups form one conjugacy orbit under
\(\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})\).

For each foreign subgroup \(H\),
\[
|N_{\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})}(H)|=32
\]
and
\[
|C_{\operatorname{Aut}(G_{60})}(H)|=16.
\]

The quotient
\[
N(H)/C(H)
\]
therefore has order two and acts nontrivially on the three
nonidentity elements of \(H\).

\subsection{Pairwise intersections}

Distinct foreign Klein groups intersect only in the identity.

There are
\[
\binom{15}{2}=105
\]
unordered subgroup pairs, and every pair satisfies
\[
H_i\cap H_j=\{1\}.
\]

Consequently, the fifteen groups contain
\[
15\cdot3=45
\]
distinct nonidentity involutions.

Each of those forty-five involutions belongs to exactly one foreign
Klein group.

\begin{proposition}
The fifteen foreign Klein groups are pairwise disjoint away from the
identity. Their nonidentity elements form a set of forty-five distinct
involutions partitioned into fifteen triples.
\end{proposition}

Thus the foreign Klein geometry is not produced by repeatedly reusing
a small collection of involutions. Each subgroup contributes its own
three nonidentity elements.

\subsection{Relay-middle chambers}

Every frontier cycle carries two external relay paths. Their two
middle vertices lie in one native quotient chamber.

For one foreign Klein group \(H\), its eight stabilized cycles do not
all use a single relay-middle chamber. Instead they divide equally
between two chambers:
\[
4+4.
\]

The initial hypothesis
\[
\text{one foreign }V_4
\longleftrightarrow
\text{one native chamber}
\]
is therefore false.

This failed correspondence is retained as a separate audit result. It
is replaced by the following two-chamber relation.

\begin{proposition}
Each foreign Klein group determines an unordered pair of native
chambers. Four of its stabilized cycles have relay middle in the first
chamber, and four have relay middle in the second.
\end{proposition}

Every foreign subgroup also has the balanced middle-relation profile
\[
4_b+4_{ab}.
\]

\subsection{The chamber-pair graph}

Construct a graph \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{for}}\) on the fifteen native
chambers. Join two chambers whenever they form the relay-middle pair
associated with one foreign Klein subgroup.

The fifteen subgroup edges are
\[
\{0,5\},\{0,14\},\{5,14\},
\]
\[
\{1,6\},\{1,12\},\{6,12\},
\]
\[
\{2,9\},\{2,13\},\{9,13\},
\]
\[
\{3,7\},\{3,11\},\{7,11\},
\]
and
\[
\{4,8\},\{4,10\},\{8,10\}.
\]

Every chamber has degree two. The graph has five connected
components, each containing three vertices and three edges.

\begin{theorem}
The foreign-Klein chamber-incidence graph is
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{for}}
\cong
5K_3.
\]
\end{theorem}

Its five components are
\[
\{0,5,14\},
\qquad
\{1,6,12\},
\qquad
\{2,9,13\},
\qquad
\{3,7,11\},
\qquad
\{4,8,10\}.
\]

Thus each three-chamber block supports exactly three foreign Klein
groups, one on each internal chamber pair.

\subsection{Cycle incidence within one triangle}

Fix one component
\[
T=\{u,v,w\}.
\]

The three foreign Klein groups correspond to
\[
\{u,v\},
\qquad
\{u,w\},
\qquad
\{v,w\}.
\]

Each subgroup stabilizes eight frontier cycles, split four and four
between its two endpoint chambers.

Accordingly, every chamber is incident with two foreign Klein groups,
and every foreign group is incident with two chambers.

The complete incidence count is
\[
15\text{ subgroups}\times8\text{ cycles}
=
120\text{ cycles}.
\]

\subsection{Carrier and foreign incidence}

The graph
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{for}}\cong5K_3
\]
is not the quotient carrier graph
\[
G_{60}/V_4\cong L(P).
\]

The same fifteen chamber labels support two different relations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item carrier adjacency, inherited from native graph edges;
    \item foreign-Klein incidence, inherited from frontier stabilizers.
\end{enumerate}

In the next section we show that the three vertices of each foreign
triangle are pairwise nonadjacent in the carrier graph. Thus the
foreign layer completes precisely those chamber triples that the
carrier keeps separate.

\subsection{Structural summary}

The frontier geometry has the exact hierarchy
\[
120
\text{ frontier cycles}
\]
\[
=
15
\text{ foreign Klein groups}
\times
8
\text{ cycles per group},
\]
with
\[
15
\text{ groups}
=
5
\text{ chamber triangles}
\times
3
\text{ edges per triangle}.
\]

The native Klein group and the foreign Klein family should not be
conflated:

\[
\boxed{
\text{native }V_4:
\text{ global free chamber register}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{foreign }V_4:
\text{ local frontier stabilizer}
}
\]

Their intersection is always trivial, and their geometric functions
are different.
